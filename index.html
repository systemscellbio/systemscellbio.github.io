Systems Cell Biology Laboratory

We aim to understand the human body as a multi-scale system: from molecular mechanisms to organelle to organ to organism.
We integrate cutting-edge techniques such as microscopy and spatial -omics to supply data-driven hypothesis generation, then follow up with mechanistic validation and clinical translation.

What we do


Spatial Biology Quantitative Imaging Cilia Mechanistic Validation AI/ML Clinical Diagnostics

We study how cells sense their environment and respond, and what happens when that machinery breaks down in disease.

Every cell is a highly structured system: proteins assemble into specific machines, subcellular structures are precisely positioned. This allows signals to travel through defined routes and pathways, and ultimately, the cell to compute responses. We use quantitative imaging and microscopy to visualize these processes directly, integrate such spatial biology data to generate hypotheses, and validate them with mechanistic biology tools. A particular focus is the primary cilium, the cell's antenna and key to understanding how cells interpret their environments.

We work across scales: from the molecular networks that build a single organelle to how their (dys)function propagates to tissue- and organ-level consequences. Collaborating closely with clinical and translational researchers, we connect our tools and mechanistic findings to patient contexts, paving new avenues for diagnostics and treatments.
