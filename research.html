Research

Research Approach

Our research closes the loop between computation and experiment: we mine complex biological datasets to propose hypotheses, then test them directly in tissue and cell models, often opening the next loop.

The Cell's Antenna

How do cells know their role in collectively building a living being? Here, we focus on the cell's antenna: the primary cilium, an antenna-like organelle built by many cell types, which indeed functions as a cellular antenna. By understanding its architecture and dynamics, we aim to reveal how cells perceive their environment and dynamically adapt.

From Molecules to Cellular Systems to Tissues

The primary cilium is particularly interesting from a systems perspective. It is likely the most heterogeneous and cell-customized organelle. Cells tune their cilia for cell-type specific sensation of their environment. We know individual signaling pathways enacted through the cilium, and we know organ-scale consequences of defect cilia. Far less understood are the manifold cell-specific roles of cilia. We study how molecular-scale organization scales up to whole-cell behavior and tissue function, integrating imaging and other data that profile this role at different biological scales.

What Goes Wrong In Disease

Defects at the molecular and cellular level can manifest as tissue- and organ-level dysfunction in human disease. We collaborate with clinical and translational partners to connect our data-driven and mechanistic tools and findings to disease-relevant contexts and patient specimens, tightly linking our work to physiological and clinical impact.

Beyond Cilia

The primary cilium is a prime example of a dynamic subcellular structure with many poorly understood roles. We are eager to extend our tools and approaches to other organelles and subcellular structures as new questions arise. By bridging disciplines and scales to connect mechanistic and clinical research, we want to pave avenues for new diagnostics and therapies.

Technology

We tackle research challenges by developing new technology and adapting existing methods. Subcellular questions demand new spatial approaches, so we build tools to comprehensively profile tissues, cells, and organelles across scales, in both fixed and live samples. We embrace the AI era by developing and customizing responsible, transparent AI tools. Here, interdisciplinarity and teamwork are essential: we involve all stakeholders (developers, scientists, clinicians, and patients) from the start, prioritizing ethical application from the very beginning.
