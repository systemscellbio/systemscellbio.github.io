Tools & Software

Open-source ImageJ/Fiji plugins developed for automated, reproducible image analysis.

Quantify ciliary shape, length, and fluorescence in 2D, 3D, and 4D images.

CiliaQ is a package of open-source ImageJ plugins for high-throughput analysis of static or time-lapse fluorescence images of cilia, determining parameters for morphology, length, bending, orientation, and fluorescence intensity.

Quantify dynamics, morphology, and fluorescence of microglia (and other cells) in 2D/3D.

MotiQ is an open-source toolbox of ImageJ plugins to quantify the motility, morphology, and fluorescence of microglia, macrophages, or any other cells or subcellular structures. It computes 60+ parameters and works with in vivo, ex vivo, and in vitro data from confocal, epifluorescence, or two-photon microscopy.

Quantify adipocyte morphology and function in tissue sections and in vitro.

AdipoQ is a simple, open-source toolbox of two ImageJ plugins to quantify adipocyte morphology and function, in tissue sections and in vitro, after histological or immunofluorescent labeling. It is compatible with different imaging modalities, supports batch processing of large datasets, and combines readouts from multiple fluorescence channels.

Included plugins: AdipoQ_Preparator (segment adipocytes, lipid droplets, or nuclei from background) and AdipoQ_Analyzer (quantify objects — count, size, and intensity within and around each object across all channels). R markdown templates are provided for post-hoc analysis.

Analyze pixel-intensity oscillations in time-lapse 2D images — e.g., ciliary beat frequency.

FreQ is an ImageJ plugin that extracts periodic changes in pixel intensity, such as those evoked by ciliary beating, and analyzes them using a Fast Fourier Transform to determine paramters like the (ciliary beat) frequency. It is well suited to characterize the ciliary beat of multi-ciliated cells in time-lapse bright-field or phase-contrast images.

Note: early versions (v0.0.1–v0.2.3) were named “Cilility_JNH”; from v0.3.0 the plugin is called FreQ.

Background & related work: FreQ's analysis was inspired by the MATLAB-based approaches in Olstad et al., Curr. Biol. 2019 and D'Gama et al., Cell Rep. 2021, which characterize motile-cilia beating in the zebrafish brain.

Comprehensively quantify the flagellar beat of sperm and sperm steering from time-lapse images.

SpermQ is a ready-to-use ImageJ plugin for detailed, automated quantification of the flagellar beat and sperm steering, from 2D time-lapse images acquired by dark-field or epifluorescence microscopy. It reconstructs the flagellum frame-by-frame and outputs head and flagellar parameters (e.g., beat frequency via Fast Fourier Transform) for both tethered and freely-swimming sperm.

Workflow: preprocess with SpermQ Preparator (background reduction, contrast, batch processing), analyze with SpermQ (fully automated), then convolve results across many flagella with SpermQ Evaluator (a pure-Java tool producing overview tables and per-flagellum PDF plots).

Multifocal Imaging Toolbox

Label-free 3D tracking of fast biological processes — including SpermQ-MF for multifocal flagellar analysis.

Multifocal Imaging Analysis Toolbox

An open-source toolbox for multifocal imaging (MFI) representing a cost-effective, label-free approach for high-speed 3D imaging. Our 3D reconstruction algorithm tracks spherical and filamentous structures across a large field of view and depth from MF images: we characterized fluid flow and the flagellar beat of human and sea urchin sperm with z-precision of 0.15 µm, in a volume of 240 × 260 × 21 µm, at 500 Hz.

Included tools: Multifocal_Preparation, MultiFocalParticleTracker-Calibrator-3, MultiFocal_AlignCurvesByLMS, MultiFocalParticleTracker-Complex-3, SpermQ-MF (analyzing flagella/filaments in multifocal images, building on SpermQ), SpermQ-MF_GenerateLUT, plus MATLAB scripts for data exploration.
